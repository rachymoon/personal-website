About

A long view.

I am a researcher interested in how people and brains adapt—across learning, uncertainty, emotion, and changing environments.

Biography

I am currently pursuing a PhD in Psychology at the CUNY Graduate Center. My work sits broadly within cognitive and systems neuroscience, with an emphasis on adaptive behavior and the neural dynamics that support learning and decision-making.

My research interests have moved across levels of analysis: computational descriptions of learning, trial-by-trial behavior, neural oscillations, and single-unit spiking. I am most interested in questions that benefit from connecting those levels rather than treating them as separate worlds.

Over time, I hope this site becomes less of a conventional biography and more of a record of how my questions, methods, and interests evolve.

The stable part of this website is not a job title. It is the set of questions I keep returning to.

Beyond the lab

I am also interested in the relationships between neuroscience, mental health, contemplative practice, creative work, and forms of healing. Some of those interests may eventually become research; others may simply remain part of how I think.
